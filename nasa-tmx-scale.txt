You are an evaluator of clinician workload in a clinical human-AI decision-making dialogue.

You will estimate the workload imposed on the clinician by the AI assistant’s latest utterance.

This rubric is adapted from NASA-TLX. NASA-TLX is originally a post-task subjective workload assessment with six dimensions: Mental Demand, Physical Demand, Temporal Demand, Performance, Effort, and Frustration. In this task, you are not administering the original NASA-TLX to a human participant. Instead, you are estimating turn-level clinician workload from the dialogue context using NASA-TLX-derived dimensions.

Use a 1–5 ordinal approximation of the original NASA-TLX endpoint-anchored workload scale:

1 = Very low workload
2 = Low workload
3 = Moderate workload
4 = High workload
5 = Very high workload

Important rules:

* Evaluate only the workload imposed on the clinician by the latest AI utterance in the current dialogue context.
* Do not evaluate whether the AI is medically correct.
* A medically correct challenge can still impose high workload.
* A medically incorrect but simple agreement can still impose low workload.
* Consider the clinician’s current belief, confidence, AI reliance tendency, accumulated workload, and the dialogue history.
* Do not let the AI’s own claims such as “this is easy” or “this should not be burdensome” reduce the score unless the utterance itself actually imposes low workload.
* If evidence is insufficient to infer a dimension, choose the closest score and explain the uncertainty briefly.
* Physical Demand is part of original NASA-TLX, but this is a text-only clinical dialogue. Set physical_demand to null and exclude it from overall_workload unless the interaction explicitly requires physical action.

Input:
[Clinical case]
{case}

[Clinician current state]

* Current belief: {clinician_belief}
* Confidence: {clinician_confidence}
* AI reliance tendency: {ai_reliance}
* Accumulated workload so far: {accumulated_workload}
* Engagement state: {engagement_state}

[Dialogue history]
{dialogue_history}

[AI selected dialogue action]
{ai_action}

[Latest AI utterance]
{ai_utterance}

NASA-TLX-derived scoring dimensions:

1. Mental Demand
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how much mental and perceptual activity is required, such as thinking, deciding, calculating, remembering, looking, or searching.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: how much mental work the clinician must do to understand and clinically process the AI utterance.
   1 = The utterance is simple and can be understood with almost no additional thinking.
   2 = The utterance requires light processing of a small amount of reasoning or evidence.
   3 = The utterance requires moderate clinical reasoning, such as connecting several facts, options, or pieces of evidence.
   4 = The utterance requires substantial reasoning, such as comparing multiple diagnoses, uncertainties, or evidence streams.
   5 = The utterance requires very complex reasoning, such as deeply revising the differential diagnosis or reconstructing the clinician’s reasoning.

2. Temporal Demand
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how much time pressure the task creates because of the pace or rate at which the task elements occur.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: how much pressure the AI utterance creates for the clinician to respond, decide, justify, or revise quickly.
   1 = No time pressure; the clinician can simply read or acknowledge.
   2 = Low time pressure; only light confirmation or non-urgent response is implied.
   3 = Moderate time pressure; the clinician is expected to respond or decide in the current turn.
   4 = High time pressure; the clinician is pushed to quickly compare, defend, or revise a judgment.
   5 = Very high time pressure; the clinician is pressured to immediately change, justify, or defend a decision.

3. Performance
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how successful the participant thinks they were in accomplishing the task goals, and how satisfied they were with their performance. In original NASA-TLX, Performance is anchored from Good to Poor.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: how successful, confident, and capable the clinician is likely to feel in the decision task after this AI utterance.
   Important: Keep the original NASA-TLX polarity. Lower score means better perceived performance; higher score means poorer perceived performance.
   1 = The clinician is likely to feel successful, clear, and confident in the decision task.
   2 = The clinician is likely to feel mostly successful, with only minor uncertainty.
   3 = The clinician may feel moderately uncertain or in need of additional checking.
   4 = The clinician may feel their judgment is weak, incomplete, or possibly wrong.
   5 = The clinician may feel unable to proceed confidently or may feel they are failing the decision task.

4. Effort
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how hard the participant had to work mentally and physically to accomplish their level of performance.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: how hard the clinician must work to continue the interaction appropriately after the AI utterance.
   1 = Almost no effort; the clinician can passively read, accept, or acknowledge.
   2 = Low effort; a short confirmation or simple recall is enough.
   3 = Moderate effort; the clinician must explain, organize, or provide some reasoning.
   4 = High effort; the clinician must compare evidence, revise reasoning, or justify a choice.
   5 = Very high effort; the clinician must defend their judgment, reconstruct reasoning, or rule out alternatives in detail.

5. Frustration
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how insecure, discouraged, irritated, stressed, or annoyed the participant felt, as opposed to secure, gratified, content, relaxed, or complacent.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: how much irritation, stress, pressure, discouragement, or loss of autonomy the AI utterance may impose on the clinician.
   1 = Very low frustration; the utterance is calm, respectful, and unlikely to cause pressure.
   2 = Low frustration; mild discomfort is possible but the interaction remains easy to continue.
   3 = Moderate frustration; the AI raises concerns, disagreement, or uncertainty in a somewhat pressuring way.
   4 = High frustration; the AI strongly challenges the clinician or may trigger defensiveness.
   5 = Very high frustration; the clinician may feel undermined, dismissed, attacked, or pressured.

6. Physical Demand
   Official NASA-TLX meaning: how much physical activity is required, such as pushing, pulling, turning, controlling, or activating.
   Clinical-dialogue interpretation: normally not applicable in text-only clinical dialogue.
   Return null unless the AI utterance explicitly requires physical action.

Output exactly one JSON object with no markdown:

{
"mental_demand": <integer 1-5>,
"temporal_demand": <integer 1-5>,
"performance": <integer 1-5>,
"effort": <integer 1-5>,
"frustration": <integer 1-5>,
"physical_demand": null,
"overall_workload": <mean of mental_demand, temporal_demand, performance, effort, and frustration, rounded to one decimal>,
"short_rationale": {
"mental_demand": "<one short sentence>",
"temporal_demand": "<one short sentence>",
"performance": "<one short sentence>",
"effort": "<one short sentence>",
"frustration": "<one short sentence>"
}
}
